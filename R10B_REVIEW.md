# R10B_REVIEW - independent design review of round 10b (S-R10BREV, standing in for astra), 2026-10-23
Read-only. Reviewed the packet, design, config, weight rule, offline fit (all sections), micro-smoke, gate .sh/.py, `model/follower_courtesy.py`, the `wms.py` diff, `losses/follower_courtesy.py`, the shared cut-in replay, the test list, R9_YIELD, R9_STUCK_DIAG and R10_REVIEW.

**Verdict: GO-WITH-CHANGES.** Nothing blocks the launch. No config value or code needs to change tonight. The required changes are (C1) to register the reject criteria below before launch and (C2) the morning reads. The main risk is real and structural, not an lr artefact. It is containable because the head can be removed exactly: zeroing `follower_courtesy.net.4.*` gives an exact no-op.

## Findings
1. **Causality / leakage: sound.** Pairs are built from s and lane at t only (nearest lane-1 car with s_i <= s_j; the nearest owner wins). Features come from kin at t. `merge_prob` is detached. Wait comes from a carried, past-only lane age. Features are zeroed and detached without a partner. The target a[t+1] is used only as the label. The test `inputs_are_current_state_only` covers this. Minor: the wait feature is dead. Ramp cars never change into lane 2, so lane_age is the 999 sentinel and the feature is 1-exp(-16.6) = 1 in both replay and rollout (`entries.insert` does not reset lane_age, so entrants inherit the sentinel too). It acts as a bias. Harmless, but the wait dependence in R9_YIELD (-0.18 to -0.41) is not modelled.
2. **Loss / "matched control": half sound.** In absolute one-step terms the head does what it should on most partner groups. Row bias, all windows, production schedule: dA 6-15 +0.90 -> +0.29; far dA>30 +0.58 -> +0.11; d_end<10 +0.69 -> +0.24. So the "far partners -0.45 vs -0.19" overshoot is mostly an artefact of the control cars. The C3 controls keep a +0.40 base bias that only partner rows get corrected for. The partner cars themselves are closer to recorded than before. **The real failure is absolute and does not go away at convergence.** In the last ~3 s before a merge the base is close to recorded, and the head adds about -1 m/s^2:
   - Held-out, production schedule, at -2/-1.5/-1/-0.5 s: after -1.42/-1.24/-1.12/-0.88; before -0.01/+0.17/+0.24/+0.32; recorded -0.04/-0.09/-0.07/-0.00.
   - The same holds on FIT episodes after the 400-step fit (-0.98..-1.37 vs recorded -0.16..-0.42).

   Cause: the inputs cannot tell "merge imminent" apart from "waiting at the end", whose residual is heavily negative, and an unweighted residual fit averages the two. This is the confound R9_YIELD section 6 met with a matched-negative contrast and the ramp car's own acceleration a_j as an input. Option A has neither. Second-order effect at the production schedule: the sign flips on the largest group, d_end>40 (n 14,632; bias +0.04 -> +0.28), and on alongside cars (-0.07 -> +0.19). Both are positive acceleration offsets, and R9_YIELD shows no yield for alongside cars. The long fit brings both back to about 0.
3. **lr x1000 / 90 steps.** Yes, the stop point acts as an uncontrolled regularizer, and production data differ from the pilot (the base moves with fix B; 300 s windows). It does NOT carry the main risk, though: the tail overshoot is the same at production and at convergence, and held-out loss is 0.578 at production vs 0.617 for the long fit. **x100 is not shown to be safer.** At x100 the held-out dA 6-15 head is -0.82 (x1000: -0.53), i.e. more near-field braking, and its event profile was not measured. Keep x1000. Do not switch on an unmeasured hunch.
4. **rollout_grad=false: sound.** `functional_call` with detached parameters keeps the state adjoint, so temporal and gate gradients through the head's forward are correct, while rollout and cut-in losses cannot turn the head into a generic brake. Tests cover both directions. Caveat: fix-B *control* rows are all lane-1 HDV rows, partner rows included, and they see a_pre + head. So temporal is also trained to cancel head errors on the same pre-merge rows: a joint fit on one shared residual. Attribution between head and fix B is therefore not clean. Zeroing the head in the morning gives "round 10 + compensation", not exactly round 10.
5. **Exact zero / CAV / one path: OK.** `torch.where(has_partner & learned, raw, 0)` is applied after the head. The loss mask uses `hdv`, with `is_cav` cut to n_steps+1. A CAV target follower shields the HDV behind it (geometric, acceptable). `merge_prob` is now computed whenever the head is on; replay and rollout share `initial_lane_age`. Tests cover flag off (bit-identical), exact no-op at init, and window > unroll. Separate note: the merged S-FIX9 change makes `step()` honour `cav_teacher` on the first-passage path, which changes recorded-arm eval replays. **Re-score round 9 with the current code** before comparing.
6. **Merge rule + fix B in rollouts.** Expected effect: pre-merge follower braking plus softer post-insertion braking, so braking moves earlier, more follower-side gaps open, and the pivot licenses more merges. That is the intended fix for stuck cars, but it can over-shoot into upstream braking waves and turn the R9_STUCK bistability the other way. Pairing switches are discrete: the offset steps by up to ~1 m/s^2 when a lane-1 car passes the ramp car or the ramp car merges, which shows up as jerk and hard-brake counts. The positive offsets (+0.25..0.39) on far and alongside followers reduce headway at the moment of insertion, so watch TTC and min gap.
7. **Gate: sufficient for mechanics, blind to behaviour.**
   - Inherits the hardened round-10 gate. R10 B1 is fixed: continuations are correct.
   - Checks courtesy finite, positive and rows>0; that the only new init tensors are the head; strict reload with the head on and rollout_grad off; frozen + pivot bitwise; head read-out moved.
   - It cannot see the tail or the size of the head output. That is acceptable for a smoke gate; the morning reads cover it.
   - `max_hours` is not enforced in run mode (R10_REVIEW), so confirm all 90 steps ran.
8. **Weight rule: sound and nearly inert.** Gradient-norm parity at 4.719; the per-batch spread is 5x. Adam is scale invariant per parameter, so the weight acts only through the global clip, where it can scale down temporal and gate steps on high-courtesy batches, and through the logged total. Do not compare training totals with round 9 or 10.

- **C2 (morning, mandatory).** Score on unseen walls/PI per protocol, never on sealed data. Three arms: round 9 (re-scored with current code), 10b, and 10b with the head zeroed (exact ablation).
