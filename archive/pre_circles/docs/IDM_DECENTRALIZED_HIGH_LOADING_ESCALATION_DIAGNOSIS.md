# IDM Decentralized High-Loading Escalation Diagnosis

Status: development-only operating-domain evidence. This report does not claim
that a damping checkpoint or wall-escalation predictor works.

## Question

The intended architecture assigns low-authority, decentralized damping to
states where a scattered CAV can safely absorb a developing wave. Coordinated
moving-wall control is reserved for states where verified damping remains
insufficient. This diagnosis asks whether the poor highest-loading outcomes are
explained by missing treatment dose/projection or by residual traffic risk after
substantial damping authority was actually delivered.

The analysis uses the 114 already opened fresh p30 paired opportunities from
seeds 4121--4132 at 1900, 2000, 2100 and 2200 veh/h. Configured demand is only a
reporting stratum; it is not treated as a deployable critical-density threshold.

## Delivered authority

Across all 114 opportunities:

- 93.0% received at least 16 of 20 command steps;
- 94.7% passed safety and service constraints excluding dose;
- median command projection survival was 1.00;
- 90.4% of command steps preserved at least half of the requested change from
  the analytical nominal command.

At 2200 specifically, adequate dose was 87.5%, safety/service admissibility
excluding dose was 100%, median projection survival was 1.00, and 91.7% of
command steps preserved at least half the requested change. The poor response
therefore cannot be attributed primarily to the controller never engaging or
to the hard shield erasing its commands.

## Outcome changes with loading

The qualified p30 response was heterogeneous and not monotone enough to justify
a configured-demand switch:

- 1900: mean +0.0715, tenth percentile -0.0645, qualified harmful fraction
  13.8%;
- 2000: mean +0.0192, tenth percentile -0.1074, qualified harmful fraction
  16.7%;
- 2100: mean +0.0268, tenth percentile -0.0809, qualified harmful fraction
  30.8%;
- 2200: mean -0.0159, tenth percentile -0.1674, qualified harmful fraction
  38.1%.

The 2200 predecision states also had a different realized risk history. Median
short-horizon severe-braking EMA was 2.63, compared with 0.26, 0.31 and 0.30 at
1900, 2000 and 2100. Median severe-braking trend was 0.486 at 2200 versus
approximately 0.052--0.059 in the lower cells. These causal, predecision
history fields are more defensible escalation inputs than configured demand.

## Scientific interpretation

The evidence supports a bounded role for decentralized damping:

1. At lower loading it has measurable beneficial authority, although a learned
   or conservative gate is still required because harmful cases remain.
2. As realized severe-braking exposure and trend increase, the same fully
   delivered p30 action increasingly has an adverse or insufficient tail.
3. Stronger braking is not the warranted response: the existing terminal-speed
   action already survives projection on most command steps, and a larger speed
   differential risks creating a downstream shock.
4. After a damping checkpoint is independently verified, high residual-risk
   states should be labeled for a separate counterfactual comparison of
   identical damping versus identical damping plus a temporary moving wall.

This is an escalation-domain argument, not proof that the wall succeeds. The
future predictor must estimate the conditional benefit of escalation from
realized patterns and treatment feasibility; it must not learn a hard
configured-demand threshold.

## Collapse limitation

Neither p30 nor abstention changed the maximum consecutive collapse-condition
duration in any of these 114 opportunities. These rows therefore contain no
collapse-prevention headroom and cannot support a collapse-prevention claim.
The current defensible damping outcomes are severe braking, acceleration-energy
response, TTC and bounded service effects. If collapse prevention remains a
primary wall claim, a separate IDM-native headroom pilot must first demonstrate
both collapse-prevented and collapse-not-prevented outcomes under valid wall
dose.
