# Proposed experiment sequence after the brake/HOLD audit

Owner request: plan what to try, allowing a longer map and accepting on-ramp CAV damping. This document is a proposed sequence, not a simulation registration or authorization for an automatic campaign. No new experiment was launched to prepare it. Historical protocols, original labels, checkpoints and sealed data remain unchanged.

The objective is a CAV-report-based RSU policy that chooses a useful, executable moving wall over continued frozen CIRCLES damping. More reliable rejection alone is useful engineering but does not establish positive escalation value. The old treatment audits and current acquired-report reconstruction are complete and should be reused.

## Common contract

Keep exact CIRCLES inference, sensing/ownership boundaries and native handback. On-ramp CAV damping remains enabled; HDVs receive no commands. Retain a fixed mainline cohort and genuine two-lane compact HOLD. Do not silently substitute fixed-band metering, lane staggering, tau changes or multiwall.

Retain all-request service, source waiting, measured braking, squared acceleration effort, TTC, collision involvements, teleports and unfinished requests over a common complete horizon. Preserve the original materiality/guardrail labels. Additionally report joint non-deterioration in the five continuous costs, with zero treatment safety events; do not relabel old failures. Zero-valued safety metrics and already-incurred waiting need not strictly decrease. Prior incidents remain disclosed separately from treatment consequences.

Every new wall variant is frozen before its outcomes and is compared against the same-scenario damping reference and the preceding wall version. Reuse verified references and exact prefixes where possible. Each physical block has an explicit source/scenario/seed registry, zero automatic retries and no automatic successor. Aborts, refusals and incomplete service remain outcomes. A refusal is not an observed counterfactual label for the wall that was never attempted.

## A. Decide whether the road or its control region actually needs changing

Question: is the constraint the release boundary, the recruitment/control region, downstream traffic, or evaluation truncation?

Use the existing trajectories, acquired-report audit and network geometry; budget zero simulations. Calculate remaining assembly/HOLD/exit distance with the actual acceleration ramp. Separate map-boundary reserve from traffic-relative clearance. The latest episodes served every requested trip by 2400 seconds, so there is no current evidence of horizon truncation.

The historical v5.7 extension added 900 m outside its eligible region and was not physically evaluated as that derived-network design. Do not repeat that mistake. If usable road is insufficient, specify one local derived map/control-region version with its length calculated from the maneuver requirement; do not sweep lengths. Preserve the shared original. Verify connectivity, lane mapping, routes, speed limits, bottleneck/ramp geometry and the controller's eligibility on added edges. Do not assume physical extension automatically extends controller authority.

Freeze the scenario before efficacy. Both damping and wall must use the new map. Added free-flow time, storage and shifted merge arrivals are scenario effects, not wall benefit. Old-map damping is not a new-map reference. Any claimed comparison across maps must report within-map wall-minus-damping effects and disclose changed predecision traffic; it does not isolate length causally by itself.

## B. Repair the whole maneuver, retaining the intended gentle contract

Question: can a selected cohort assemble, hold and return control under one consistent finite commitment?

Keep -0.3 m/s² voluntary braking, 0.15 m/s³ command ramp and a 20-second maximum HOLD as the repair reference, not as proven optimal settings. Define an explicit finite exit and terminal handback condition. Account for the ramp and only the remaining phases. Evaluate the same commitment at entry and during execution using received reports. Do not merely shift the old 78–107-second constant-speed stopping envelope into admission, or invent an IDM/native acceleration for tapering.

Mechanics budget: at most two 120-second fixtures, one initially accelerating entry-to-exit case and one developing downstream constraint during HOLD. Verify nonvacuous maneuver delivery in the feasible fixture, timely response in the changing fixture, sampled physical responses, command bounds and exact handback. A command cap is not a measured acceleration guarantee under SUMO protection.

Full physical check on the existing 4107/4108 M2700/t600 cases: on the unchanged map, two new 2400-second wall episodes with verified reused damping, for a total block ceiling of 5040 seconds including mechanics. If A instead selects a changed map, register two new damping/wall pairs on that map. Choose and freeze their common horizon before execution, with a proposed cap of 3600 seconds per episode; ceiling 14400 corridor seconds plus 240 mechanics seconds. If a justified horizon exceeds that cap, revise the budget before launching, not during a run.

Decision: all refusal means no positive wall headroom, even if rejection improves. Correct delivery with physical harm means mechanics repair is insufficient. Useful qualifying intervention supports replication, not immediate model fitting. An implementation defect stops the block; a physical negative is preserved as the answer.

## C. Test one mechanism-directed alternative if B lacks useful benefit

Do not launch all alternatives as a grid. Choose one using the first consequential interaction and information acquired before its decision. Proposed ceiling: four full episodes on existing development cases, plus at most two 120-second targeted mechanics fixtures if the new behavior needs them. Register the exact selection, horizon and comparison before execution. No justified mechanism means zero new runs.

Priority C1, placement/cohort: test whether a report-selected cohort encounters the relevant slowdown at a useful place and time with lower assembly cost. Change cohort/location selection only; retain the wall law and other limits. Predecision regional and neighbor reports must support the selection. An exact unobserved queue tail, hidden vehicle identities or future arrivals cannot be inputs. Compare against the previous selection on the same scenario and damping realization; changing cohorts is a selection-policy change, not a pure initial-span experiment.

Priority C2, target-speed attainability: use only if measured target pursuit is the remaining problem. Test one bounded target rule based on acquired lane/downstream motion, retaining cohort selection, braking contract and duration. It must handle missing reports and uncertain distant slowdowns without turning a distant stopped vehicle into an instantaneous stop target. Assess compactness, formation cost, all traffic costs and delayed harms. Do not repeat the historical 10/20/30-percent sweep.

Braking authority is conditional, not a separate default sweep. If acquired and measured evidence demonstrates that required cross-lane deceleration exceeds 0.3, first determine whether a different feasible cohort/target avoids that demand. If no gentle cohort can deliver the intended useful maneuver, explicitly document the tradeoff and specify one physically justified alternative bound for a separate test. The old 0.3/1.0 and current 0.3/3.0 comparisons already establish that globally gentler or stronger is not uniformly better. Do not automatically repeat stronger assembly followed by gentle HOLD, which was also tried historically.

## D. Test duration after formation and exit can actually be delivered

Question: when is continuing coordination better than beginning release?

Use a frozen, executable controller. Retain H0/H20 as existing causal questions, and test nominal H40 only when it can actually be delivered. A short controlled fixture may establish availability, not efficacy. On an unchanged valid implementation, reuse exact old cells; a changed lifecycle needs newly matched outcomes.

Prefer one proposal-bound continuation rule versus fixed H20, using remaining feasibility, measured progress and acquired compression/downstream response. It may terminate inside a declared maximum commitment. This is a new dynamic policy, not proof that old adaptive health/discharge rules worked. Existing records already show several such rules were inert or acted too late.

Proposed diagnostic ceiling: six full branches, at most two same-prefix branch points with release-now, continue-to-fixed-end and the frozen alternative. Every branch is generated by ordinary acquired-only policy execution; matched future outcomes are offline evidence. Count all actual replay/prefix simulation work in registration. Do not infer the value of an unexecuted release from the continuation trajectory alone. If the common-prefix counterfactual runner is not verified, use full episode arms instead. Select exactly which comparisons the six branches support before launching; no implicit factorial expansion.

## E. Check repeatability before creating a large training set

Advance only if a frozen wall has material physical value in development. Predeclare its intended observable operating domain and include contrasting negative conditions. Exclude seed/true demand/penetration as online features; experimental strata are offline metadata.

Proposed first replication ceiling: six exposure-audited new development seeds at two predeclared traffic settings, damping/wall pairs, 24 full episodes. This is a bounded screening panel, not a statistically powered final study or a universal penetration claim. Determine the registered horizon and absolute simulated-second cap from the chosen scenario before launch. Freeze the wall, selector and criteria throughout; keep these development seeds separate from later calibration/prospective data.

If benefits do not repeat or harms persist, stop before predictor-scale collection. Do not change the rule halfway through or search subgroups after observing outcomes and call that validation.

## F. Determine whether acquired observations identify useful actions

First perform an offline headroom and observation audit on valid version-matched data: zero new simulations. Retain original physical labels and also inspect continuous cost vectors and joint non-deterioration. Group whole seeds; equivalent branches and snapshots are not independent evidence. If no wall qualifies in a state, a retrospective selector's correct choice is damping.

Inputs are acquired regional history, candidate-member geometry/response, downstream report evidence, missingness/age, and the proposed action parameters. Predict feasibility and individual physical differences conditional on that proposal. Do not use true traffic state, future shadows or a single unqualified health score. Admission and continuation are different decision datasets; post-entry signals cannot become entry features.

Compare a transparent physical rule and a small action-conditional model against continued damping, feasibility-only escalation and a fixed wall rule. Include all attempted actions and downstream outcomes in policy value. More complex temporal models require new evidence of a missing signal; old overfitting results do not justify trying them again by default.

If action contrast is still missing, propose at most one separately registered block of 12 additional wall branches using existing matched references and at most two mechanism-supported profiles. This is a capped identifiability pilot, not an assurance that 12 rows can train or calibrate a selector. If the labels/observations remain insufficient, report that result before further collection.

## G. Freeze and evaluate the paper claim

Only after repeatable headroom and held-development decision value: register separate training, calibration and prospective groups after auditing prior exposure. Choose final sample size from observed variability and the declared precision target; no invented publication probability or arbitrary powered-sample claim. Each stage's cost is approved/registered before execution; the final sample budget is intentionally not guessed now.

Prospective comparison: damping versus the frozen observable escalation policy, with the transparent rule as a comparator. Use identical scenarios, all-request accounting and full horizons. Report overall policy value, benefits and harms by predeclared condition, refusal rate, successful wall delivery and costs of failed attempts. Keep all original guardrails and report strict joint non-deterioration separately.

Robustness follows in separate bounded blocks: CAV availability/penetration, receipt delay and missing reports, sensor noise/occlusion assumptions, and driver/traffic variation. Test feature acquisition and abstention offline first; only surviving meaningful degradations need full traffic outcomes. Do not cross every robustness factor into a huge grid.

Draft the methods and negative-result history now. The final efficacy claim requires prospective evidence; mechanics, retrospective selection and reward curves cannot replace it.

## Immediate priority

Execute A as a no-simulation scenario decision, then implement/register B. C and D are conditional design experiments, E is replication, F is observation-based selection, and G is paper validation. These are separate budgets, not one large campaign. Stop once evidence answers a question; do not automatically consume the next block.
