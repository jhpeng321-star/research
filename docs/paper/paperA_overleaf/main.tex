\begin{abstract}
MAPPO-style multi-agent learning can teach connected and automated vehicles
(CAVs) competent individual driving while missing rare cooperative moves.
Walls, gap creation and metering may pay off minutes later, when variation
from human drivers, arrivals and other CAVs obscures credit in a shared return.
We propose a physics-informed world model (WM) that evaluates paired
counterfactuals from observable state: the group acts, each member acts alone,
and all members follow baseline. Reusing the same sampled hidden futures
can cancel shared noise and reveal excess-over-solos cooperation credit.
The model needs useful action and credit rankings rather than a reconstruction
of the single realized future. Existing exact-model planning establishes
headroom: it saves
% source: E:/agent-work/c4-coopplan/docs/paper_results/COOPPLAN.md:22--27.
$648.407$ $[209.699,1173.616]$ and $1583.821$ $[588.246,2765.414]$
veh-s/world at the J600 and J900 clocks on $36$ development worlds.
Starting from MAPPO, we ramp in WM replay credit and compare against both
frozen MAPPO and same-budget continued training.
\pending{credit fidelity, variance reduction and learned-policy gains /
S-COOPMWM; insert final outcome sentence only after complete evidence.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% Editorial allocation: I 0.60 page; front matter 0.35 page.
\para{Cooperation pays late.}
A CAV that slows down may initially worsen its own progress while creating a
gap, metering an arrival stream or helping stabilize a cross-lane wall.
The group benefit can emerge minutes later. A shared traffic reward must
assign that delayed benefit amid changes caused by hidden human behaviour,
new arrivals and other agents. Competent individual driving therefore need
not reveal how to coordinate rare interventions. This motivates a credit
assignment hypothesis, not a general impossibility claim about MARL.
In an existing, deliberately oversampled TRAIN corpus, helpful options occur in
% source: E:/agent-work/c4-coopbandit/docs/paper_results/COOPBANDIT.md:7--18.
$45/300$ states: $15\%$, with a $95\%$ world-bootstrap interval of
$[10,20.33]\%$. Only $81$ states have an eligible group.
This is corpus prevalence, not an on-road event rate.

\para{Compare futures instead of predicting reality.}
A physics-informed WM can compare interventions under a shared uncertain
future without reproducing the future that actually occurred. Paired joint,
member-alone and baseline branches expose the component of a group benefit
that exceeds the sum of solo benefits. Common random numbers (CRN) can cancel
background variation. The actor still collects and executes trajectories in
real SUMO; the WM supplies auxiliary credit. Starting from MAPPO and ramping
that credit is intended to preserve individual competence while making
cooperation learnable. \pending{measured noise cancellation, ranking fidelity
and emergent cooperation / S-COOPMWM.}

\section{Related Work}
% Editorial allocation: II 0.45 page. All keys have inherited verification receipts.
\para{Counterfactual credit.}
Difference-reward lineage~\cite{wolpert_tumer2002}, COMA~\cite{foerster_coma2018}
and difference-reward policy gradients~\cite{castellini_drreinforce}
establish credit by counterfactual subtraction. Our group excess-over-solos
quantity differs from an agent's marginal-in-team contribution and from a
Shapley allocation~\cite{han_shapley2022}. Contextual counterfactual planning
also measures cooperation~\cite{barnett_counterfactual2026}. The target
contribution is the traffic-specific long-horizon paired-replay learning and
verification pipeline, not invention of the subtraction equation.

\para{World-model imagination.}
Dreamer-style imagined learning is relevant
\citeneeded{Dreamer primary paper; no verified inherited entry}.
MACD, the Multi-Agent Counterfactual Dreamer, is direct prior art for
model-based counterfactual credit~\cite{chai_macd2024}.
Physics-informed traffic MARL is likewise established~\cite{liu_physics_wm2026}.
Here WM output supplies credit while trajectories come from real SUMO,
reducing direct dependence on model-generated training transitions.
Action-response validity still needs testing~\cite{oefinger_validate2026};
the actor can exploit a biased auxiliary credit even without imagined
transition training.

\para{Mixed-autonomy cooperation.}
MAPPO~\cite{yu_mappo2022}, on-ramp MARL~\cite{chen_merge2021} and
Flow~\cite{wu_flow2022} provide the learning and mixed-traffic lineage.
CIRCLES~\cite{lee_circles2024} demonstrates open-road wave-control context;
AV coalition rewards~\cite{han_shapley2022} provide cooperation incentives.
Platoon and merge coordination are related mechanisms
\citeneeded{dedicated platoon reference if a specific prior claim is added}.
Coordinated appearance and aggregate return do not by themselves establish
excess-over-solos benefit.

\section{Problem Formulation}
% Editorial allocation: III 0.65 page.
\subsection{Partially Observed Multi-CAV Control}
Define a variable-agent Dec-POMDP with latent traffic state $x_t$, transition
law $P$, active CAV set $\mathcal A_t$, causal local histories $h_{i,t}$,
RSU observations $z_t$, and shared traffic reward. A decentralized actor uses
local history and an observable RSU broadcast; a centralized critic is used
only during training. Hidden driver behaviour and future arrivals are
uncertainty, not actor inputs. The WM samples a fitted/prior-conditioned
future $\xi\sim q(\xi\mid h,z)$ rather than the realized hidden future.
Residual speed and time-gap setpoints are anchored to MAPPO commands; lane
behaviour and the safety executor retain the inherited plant contract.

\subsection{Source-Inclusive Team Delay}
For a baseline/intervention $S$, use a speed-deficit delay integral
\begin{equation}
J_H(S)=\int_t^{t+H}\!\left[\sum_{j\in\mathcal V(\tau)}
\left(1-\frac{v_j(\tau)}{v^{\rm free}_j}\right)_+
+Q_{\rm src}(\tau)\right]\,d\tau .
\label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal V$ contains all on-road vehicles and $Q_{\rm src}$ counts
requested vehicles waiting to enter. Future entrants and source waiting
remain in the team objective, in veh-s; lower is better.
Scoring references and source requests are not additional actor observations.
\pending{bind exact evaluator free-speed and source-wait convention /
S-COOPMWM.}
Distinguish intervention duration $W$, replay horizon $H$, and episode score
clocks. The existing planner's
% source: E:/agent-work/c4-coopplan/docs/paper_results/COOPPLAN.md:14--15,22--30,73--84.
J600/J900 clocks start after a $300$ s warmup; event credit uses H120 or,
in separately reported panels, H300.

\subsection{Exact Cooperation Estimand}
Freeze group $G$, background $B$, joint proposed targets, nonmember actions,
intervention window and continuation. In branch $S\subseteq G$, only members
of $S$ issue their joint-proposed targets; other group members follow $B$.
Solo branches retain joint targets and context rather than replanning.
\begin{align}
D_H(S)&=J_H(B)-J_H(S),\\
\cg&=D_H(G)-\sum_{i\in G}D_H(\{i\})\label{eq:synergy}\\
&=\sum_{i\in G}J_H(\{i\})-J_H(G)-(|G|-1)J_H(B).\nonumber
\end{align}
Beneficial cooperation requires $D_H(G)>0$ and $\cg>0$. Positive synergy
alone can coexist with a harmful group. For larger groups, $C_G$ aggregates
beyond-singleton interactions; it is neither a Shapley allocation nor intent.
Member-removal marginals $J(G\setminus\{i\})-J(G)$ are not solo effects.

\subsection{MAPPO Residual Policy and RSU Broadcast}

Trainable residual speed/log-time-gap setpoints and a sensed-traffic RSU
broadcast supply coordination capacity. Zero residual must reproduce MAPPO's
command execution, not just a neural output. The inherited design proposes
% source: E:/agent-work/c4-coopnative/docs/paper_results/COOPMWM_DESIGN.md:3--14.
a four-dimensional zero-initialized broadcast and $10$ s windows.
\pending{final policy, residual tracking, critic and input/capacity freeze.}
Native lanes, command ownership and safety clamping are shared across arms;
report realized execution rather than interpreting every changed command as
a changed physical action.

\subsection{Paired Counterfactual Replay from Observable State}
At a real SUMO decision, freeze the causal observable start, group and targets.
Sample complete hidden-driver/arrival futures from the fitted population and
source prior. The inherited backend uses
% source: E:/agent-work/c4-coopnative/docs/paper_results/COOPMWM_DESIGN.md:15--23.
$K=8$ draws. For each draw $\xi_k$, reuse identity-bound latent parameters and
arrivals across baseline, joint and member-alone branches. Nonmember targets
and continuation are identical. Missing futures invalidate the paired set;
they are not silently removed. Compute
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\widehat C_{G,H}=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\Big[&
\sum_i\widehat J_H^{(k)}(\{i\})-\widehat J_H^{(k)}(G)\\
&-(|G|-1)\widehat J_H^{(k)}(B)\Big].
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Ranking fidelity is task-specific. A nonlinear monotone cost transform can
preserve action order while changing synergy subtraction; hence action-delay
rank alone cannot qualify $\widehat C_G$. Check exact credit sign/rank too.

\subsection{Noise Cancellation and Multi-Horizon Qualification}
For branch costs $X,Y$,
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Var}(X-Y)=\operatorname{Var}(X)+\operatorname{Var}(Y)
-2\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y).
\end{equation}
For the synergy contrast with coefficient vector $a$, variance is
$a^\top\Sigma a$. CRN can reduce variance when paired branches share
positively correlated variation. It does not remove model bias or guarantee
that most noise cancels. Compare CRN replay, independently sampled replay
and real team-return advantage at matched starts/groups/horizons, reporting
raw variance, standardized signal and world-level uncertainty. Zero or biased
credit is not useful merely because its variance is small.
\pending{variance reduction with preserved exact signal / S-COOPMWM.}

\subsection{Exact Playback Check and Credit Ramp}
Full-prefix exact SUMO reruns check no-op/trajectory parity, controller memory,
shared exogenous coupling, joint/solo identity and source bookkeeping.
Use sampled TRAIN windows for calibration/verification; exact costs must not
become actor features or optimization reward labels. Prior saved-state
mechanics failures motivate contract-specific prefix checks, not assumed
exactness from a common seed.

The intended learning rule retains PPO/MAPPO-style real team-return learning
and adds ramped WM cooperation credit. A proposed aggregate auxiliary form is
\begin{align}
q_G&=w_{\rm ind}\sum_i\widehat D_H(\{i\})
+w_{\rm coop}\widehat C_{G,H},\\
&=w_{\rm coop}\widehat D_H(G)
+(w_{\rm ind}-w_{\rm coop})\sum_i\widehat D_H(\{i\}),\nonumber
\end{align}
with $0\leq w_{\rm coop}\leq w_{\rm ind}$. This preserves nonnegative
expanded singleton weight algebraically; it does not guarantee a good policy.
\pending{per-CAV allocation, score/advantage insertion, normalization, scale
and ramp under coalition-alone semantics.}
The matched control receives the same capacity and continued training with
the auxiliary credit disabled. Policies execute in real SUMO, reducing direct
transition-model exploitation while still requiring checks for reward gaming
through action-dependent credit bias.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
% Editorial allocation: VII 0.25 page.
Ranking is sufficient only for the decision/contrast being used. Delay order
does not guarantee synergy subtraction, calibrated probabilities or unbiased
credit. CRN cancels shared variation only to the extent branches covary;
hidden-state aliasing, cold controller memory and source priors remain.
Member-alone and member-removal definitions must be reconciled, and exact
prefix parity must be verified for each replay contract.

Positive synergy with harmful group delay is not beneficial cooperation.
Real SUMO execution still permits auxiliary-reward gaming; capacity/input/
budget-matched controls and service/safety outcomes are essential. The
finite-option oracle is an empirical upper reference with privileged
prediction, not an optimum. Selected examples, different development panels,
a single merge plant and limited training seeds constrain inference.
\pending{final exposure/freeze, reproducibility and seed-variability evidence.}

\section{Conclusion}
